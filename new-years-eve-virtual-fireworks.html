Virtual fireworks for New Year’s Eve

Two ways to have fireworks at midnight without a permit, a ban or a cold night outside: a countdown greeting you can send, and an endless show for the screen.

A countdown to send

The New Year greeting is set on a frozen mountain lake at midnight. Snow drifts down, a full moon hangs over the peaks, and a village on the far shore has every window lit. Gold ground fireworks play along the ice, and a patch of open water mirrors them. Then a giant glowing clock rises over the lake and counts down from ten, and each number is a tick you can hear.

At zero the sky goes off. Shells that have been climbing since the countdown began all break together across the whole sky, the ground fireworks erupt, the windows across the lake flare, and the clock turns into the new year in gold sparks. Your words come next, then their name, under a curtain of falling gold.

An endless show for the party screen

Open the live show and leave it running. It puts on fireworks over a beach at dusk and designs itself as it goes: volleys, sweeps across the sky, mirrored pairs, a pause before a big volley, a finale now and then. Every minute or so it changes its colors and shells, and sometimes its view. It doesn’t repeat, and no words go up, so it works as a backdrop for a room.

It starts with the sound off, so it won’t surprise a quiet room. A button at the bottom of the screen turns it on. It also asks your device to keep the screen awake where the browser allows it, which helps on a TV or a phone on a stand. It isn’t a broadcast of a city’s countdown. It’s a made-up show that runs as long as you leave it on, which is what you want from a screen at a party.

Why virtual fireworks

Real fireworks are loud, they frighten some pets and people, and in dry or windy places they’re restricted or banned. A screen show has no bang to brace for, no smoke to clear and nothing to sweep up. It isn’t the same as standing under a real display, and we won’t pretend it is, but it’s a good addition to a party, a good stand-in when the real show has been cancelled, and a good way to include the people who can’t be there.

Tips for a screen party

  • Put the browser in full screen, dim the lights, and use the biggest screen you have.
  • Connect a laptop to the TV, or mirror your phone or computer screen to it.
  • Turn the sound on for the countdown: the ticks and the bell at midnight are part of it.
  • Keep a second device handy to send the greeting to someone who’s watching from far away.
