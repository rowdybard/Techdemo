Say thank you with golden fireworks

Gold willows fall like slow light over the water while your thank-you spells out in the sky. A small, memorable way to thank someone properly.

What the show looks like

The thank-you greeting is warm and golden. Willows fall in long strands of light, palms open over the water, and then your words are spelled across the sky with the name of the person you’re thanking underneath. It’s a show that takes its time.

Who to thank

The people who are easy to forget to thank: a teacher at the end of term, the host after a dinner, a coworker who covered for you, the neighbor who watered the plants, a coach, a mentor, a nurse, a friend who showed up when it mattered. A thank-you note is lovely. A thank-you they can watch and replay is something they’ll tell someone about.

What to write

Up to 24 characters on the message line, plus an optional second line. Use the second line for the reason; a thank-you lands harder when it’s specific.

  • THANK YOUThe plain version, which is often the best one.
  • THANKS, COACHAdd “FOR THE LIFTS ALL YEAR” on the second line.
  • THANK YOU, MS. LEEWith the year or the class underneath.
  • YOU MADE MY DAYFor the small kindness that stayed with you.
  • IT MEANT A LOTAdd “FOR COVERING MY SHIFT”, or whatever it was.
  • THANKS FOR EVERYTHINGTwenty-one characters, for the person who did everything.

Timing

Send it while the favor is still fresh, the same evening if you can. If the moment has passed, send it anyway; a late thank-you is better than none, and this one has a good excuse for being dramatic.

Customize can change the colors and the sky if gold isn’t the right feeling. You can also keep it quiet, with fewer fireworks and a slower pace. Preview the show before you send it.
