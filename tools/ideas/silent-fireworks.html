Why people look for this

The bangs are the hard part. Loud, sudden noise can be difficult for pets, babies and small children, and for anyone who’s sensitive to sound. Real fireworks also come with smoke, debris and, in dry weather, a risk of fire. A show on a screen has none of that, and you decide how loud it is.

We can’t promise any show will suit every pet or person. If you’re trying it with someone who’s easily startled, start with the sound off, keep the screen at a distance, and watch how they react.

How to keep it quiet

  • The live show starts with the sound off. A button at the bottom of the screen switches it on if you want it.
  • Greetings don’t make a sound until you tap or press a key on the page, so one won’t bang when it opens. Turn your phone’s volume down, or put it on silent, and the show plays the same.
  • When you’re making one, Customize has a Sound switch under Extras, and sliders for pace, size and smoke if you want it gentler.

Calm by design

The “I love you” look is the calmest one: an early sunset, soft pastel colors, a slow pace and very little smoke. If you’re sending a quiet greeting, that’s a good place to start. In Customize, set Pace toward Calm and Size toward Small, and move the sky toward sunset for a slower, softer show.

Where real fireworks aren’t allowed

In dry or windy places, apartments, and communities with rules about noise, real fireworks can be restricted or banned, and public displays are sometimes called off. A show on a screen is a gentle stand-in: no fire, no real smoke, nothing to put out and nothing to clean up. It isn’t the same as a real display, and we wouldn’t say it is, but for a quiet evening it’s a pleasant one.

For a whole room

If you’d like fireworks on in the background, open the live show. It plays on its own, never repeats, and leaves the sound off until you turn it on. It works on a TV, a laptop, or a phone on a stand.
