How the words get into the sky

A single firework shell throws hundreds of sparks into the shape of your letters, so the words appear all at once, hang there for a moment, and then fall and fade the way sparks do. The name you type goes on a line of its own, below the message.

What fits

  • The message: up to 24 characters, shown in capital letters.
  • A second line (optional): up to 24 more, for a date, a detail or a reason.
  • Their name: up to 16 characters, on a line of its own.
  • Your name: shown on the card they see after the show, so they know who it’s from.

Accented letters such as é, ñ and ç work, so “FELIZ CUMPLEAÑOS” and “FRANÇOIS” spell out properly.

Tips for words that read well

  • Short words are easier to read in the sky than long ones. Two or three words per line is plenty.
  • Put the name on its own line rather than inside a long message.
  • Skip most punctuation. A single “!” is fine; a string of symbols gets lost.
  • Use the second line for the detail: the year, the place, the reason.
  • Press Preview the show before you send, and shorten anything that looks crowded.

Name and message ideas

  • WELCOME HOME / MIAFor someone coming back from a long trip.
  • CONGRATS GRAD / JORDANWith the school on the second line.
  • WILL YOU BE MY DATE / ALEXNineteen characters for a good question.
  • GOOD LUCK / THE BEARSFor a team before the big game.
  • THANK YOU / MR. OKAFORFor a teacher; the name line has room for sixteen characters.

See it bigger

The link works on any phone, but a name in the sky is best on a bigger screen. If you’re together, open it on a laptop or a TV. If you’re apart, they’ll still see it fill their screen, and they can tap the sky afterwards to launch fireworks of their own.
